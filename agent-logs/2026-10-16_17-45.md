fn resolve_claims(
    mesh: &ConnectedMesh,
    region_faces: &[usize],
    committed: &mut Vec<CompeteHyp>,
    claim_lists: &[Vec<usize>],
    vtol: f64,
    config: &CompeteConfig,
) -> (Vec<CompeteHyp>, Vec<usize>) {

        .iter()
        .map(|h| h.faces().iter().map(|&fi| face_area(&mesh.faces[fi], &mesh.vertices)).sum())
        .collect();

    let mut leftovers: Vec<usize> = Vec::new();

    for &fi in region_faces {
        let claimants = &claim_lists[fi];
        if claimants.is_empty() {
            leftovers.push(fi);
            continue;

            continue;

        // area, then lowest index: a face on both a cylinder and a torus near
        // their tangency is the cylinder's; a face on a real fillet and a
        // spurious thin junction torus (both sub-micron) is the larger
        // fillet's. Comparing absolute best-fit here instead would let a tiny
        // tighter sliver steal it and fragment one surface into a chain.
        let within: Vec<usize> = claimants.iter().copied().filter(|&ci| dist(ci) <= vtol).collect();

                .iter()
                .min_by(|&&a, &&b| {

                        .dof_rank()
                        .cmp(&committed[b].dof_rank())
                        .then_with(|| hyp_area[b].partial_cmp(&hyp_area[a]).unwrap())
                        .then_with(|| a.cmp(&b))

                .unwrap()

            // to leave the larger-area claimant (a true junction face fits
            // both sides equally; the margin prevents flapping).

                hyp_area[b].partial_cmp(&hyp_area[a]).unwrap().then_with(|| a.cmp(&b))
            });

                    winner = ci;
                    winner_d = d;
                }
            }

### Option C — two circles from the ring structure

Two ruling-aligned quads give six vertices in two rings of three, one per rim.
Three points in space determine a unique circle, so each rim yields a centre, a
radius and a normal; the line through the two centres is the axis and the two
radii at two heights give the half-angle by similar triangles. Exact, vertices
only, non-iterative, and it works from **2 facets**.

Measured on the seed above: axis **0.0002°** off, half-angle **0.0001°** off,
the two radii recovered to 0.1–0.2× vtol, against a mesh error of 4.1e-7 mm.
Conditioning is comfortable rather than marginal — the rings span 31.3°, and the
sensitivity \`2ε/α²\` gives a radius uncertainty of 1.1e-5 mm, under a quarter of
vtol.

**Cost and weakness.** Needs the ruling structure the other options do not: two
rings with ≥3 points each, i.e. ruling-aligned facets. Phase 1.0 measured 25.6%
of corpus cylinder facets straddling three or more rulings, so this cannot be the
only route. Ring identification needs no provisional axis — the shared edge
between two quads in a strip *is* a ruling, so its endpoints are one per ring and
walking the strip labels the rest combinatorially.

**Built 2026-10-15 as \`initialize::cone_from_rings\`; measured; not wired.** Two
departures from the design above, and the reason it stays unwired.

- *Rims are found, not labelled.* "A shared edge is a ruling" holds only for
  quads in one band; in a triangle strip the shared edges alternate between
  rulings and diagonals. Instead every split of the seed's vertices into two
  groups of ≥3 is tried — at most \`2^(n−1)\`, with \`n\` capped at 10 by a work
  bound that decides nothing — each scored by the largest distance of any vertex
  from the cone the split implies. The true split scores at the mesh's own
  precision and a wrong one misses by a wide margin. Unit-tested exact to 1e-9 at
  10°–84°, from **two quads or four triangles** alike.
- *Taper, not apex.* The profile is \`ρ = r₁ + t·h\`, so a split reading \`t = 0\`
  is a cylinder at a regular point rather than an apex at infinity (and is left
  to the caller's own cylinder route).

**Why it is not wired: two coaxial circles always lie on a cone.** A single band
of *any* surface of revolution has its vertices on two coaxial circles, so this
route finds an **exact** cone on a band of a torus fillet just as it does on a

at its rims), i.e. from competition or branching, not from a better fit.

/// Committed hypotheses that are redundant: every one of their faces is also
/// claimed, and explained within `vtol`, by a single other claimant of strictly
/// larger area. Returns `(dropped, container)` per hypothesis.
///
/// Per-face resolution alone cannot see this. It hands a face several claimants
/// explain to the lowest-DOF one, which is right at a tangency — a face on both
/// a cylinder and its fillet torus is the cylinder's — but wrong when the
/// simpler claimant is merely a *piece* of the more complex one: a cone fitted
/// to one facet band of a torus is exact on that band (two coaxial circles
/// always lie on a cone, SURFACE_FITTING.md "Option C"), and it would carve the
/// band out of the fillet. A cylinder is never wholly inside its fillet, so the
/// tangency rule is untouched; an impostor confined to a band of a larger
/// surface always is.
fn contained_hypotheses(
    mesh: &ConnectedMesh,
    committed: &[CompeteHyp],
    claim_lists: &[Vec<usize>],
    hyp_area: &[f64],
    vtol: f64,
) -> Vec<(bool, usize)> {
    (0..committed.len())
        .map(|ci| {
            let faces = committed[ci].faces();
            let Some(&first) = faces.first() else { return (false, ci) };
            claim_lists[first]
                .iter()
                .copied()
                .filter(|&cj| cj != ci && hyp_area[cj] > hyp_area[ci])
                .find(|&cj| {
                    faces.iter().all(|&fi| {
                        claim_lists[fi].contains(&cj) && max_vertex_dist(mesh, &committed[cj], fi) <= vtol
                    })
                })
                .map_or((false, ci), |cj| (true, cj))
        })
        .collect()
}

/// axis oriented from the apex toward the support.
///
/// A CAD tessellator lays a cone as one band of facets spanning its whole
/// height, so every vertex of a small seed sits on one of two circles, one per
/// rim. Three points determine a circle, so each rim gives a centre and a
/// radius; the line through the centres is the axis and the two radii at two

/// nothing about a cone).
///

/// design labels rims combinatorially by treating a shared quad edge as a

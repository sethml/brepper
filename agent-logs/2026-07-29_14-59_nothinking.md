I've read the seeding loop (`compete.rs`), the ladder (`classify.rs`), the `SurfaceType` fitters, the merge machinery, and confirmed nothing like recursive subdivision appears in the refuted-ideas list. Here's my assessment.

## Short verdict: yes, it has real merit — and it's less foreign to the codebase than it sounds

Your idea is best understood as: **make the existing ladder recursive instead of falling back to per-face seeding**. The root of your recursion already exists. `fit_validate` (classify.rs:371) does precisely your steps 1–2: fit on a stride subsample of vertices (capped at 3000 faces), validate against *all* vertices, and on a near-miss fold the worst offenders into the fit set and refit (`REFIT_ADD`/`MAX_REFIT`). What's missing is your step 3: today, when that fails, `classify_regions` gives up and hands the region to `compete_region`, which re-attempts "fit a small patch → validate by flooding" once per face. That's the 103 s. Your proposal replaces that brute-force fallback with recursion.

## One correction to the diagnosis, from the code

"Good seeds require an interior face" is right, but the *why* is largely measured already — it's in the TODO's step-5 notes:

- Seeds aren't single faces today; each grows a blind ≤32-face BFS patch (`seed_patch`). Setting `SEED_PATCH_CAP=1` was measured: stepped_block fine collapses to 24,872 per-facet surfaces. A single facet pins a plane (3 DOF) but not a free 5-DOF cylinder fit — so the blind neighbourhood exists to bootstrap *parameters*, and it fails because its contamination lives in the fit parameters even though the flood re-validates faces.
- The seed *order* is already jet-interior-first (`neighbor_jet_mismatch` ascending), and the interior-skip means that once good surfaces commit, the faces left seeding are *exactly the junction-adjacent margins* — the worst possible seeds, whose patches straddle junctions. That's the "323 NO GROWTH seeds" and much of the runtime.

So the failure isn't that interior seeds are unexplored — it's that the scheme must guess where the interior is, per face, with a blind patch. Your recursion never has to guess: **the hierarchy finds homogeneous supports by construction**. A node straddling a junction fails validation and splits; a node inside one surface validates. That's the step-5 blocker ("a bootstrap that is not a blind neighbourhood") dissolved from a different direction — you never seed at all.

## Where the merit is concrete

1. **Asymptotics.** Today: O(N) seeds × ~4 candidate types × up to 8 grow rounds × (LM fit + O(region) flood). The interior-skip is a heuristic patch on exactly this. The tree: depth ⌈log₂(N/size-floor)⌉ (~10 for the 26K-face model), validation O(N) closed-form distance evals per level, one subsampled fit per node. Rough estimate puts the whole decomposition of stepped_block fine near ~1 s vs 103 s. This is the principled answer to TODO item 2's question ("how much of the seed sweep does useful work"): only nodes on root-to-leaf paths that contain boundaries matter, and the tree visits exactly those.
2. **Constant deletion.** `SEED_PATCH_CAP`, `MAX_PATCH_DEPTH`, `PATCH_RADII`, `PATCH_TRIM_ROUNDS`, `MIN_CONDITION_FACES`, seed ordering, interior-skip — the whole blind-patch family that step 5 wants gone and measured it can't yet delete. Also most of the (d)/(d2) rescue machinery: their entire job is manufacturing a clean support for the fitter, and tree nodes are clean supports.
3. **Simplification of competition semantics.** Largest-validated-area competition exists because seeds produce *overlapping* supports. Tree nodes are disjoint supports, so per-node simplest-DOF-first ladder order suffices — which also structurally prevents the degenerate-super-model problem (a free-R torus never out-areas a cylinder, because on a cylinder node the conical rung commits first and the torus rung is never reached).
4. **Bounded worst case**, as you said: pure-NURBS regions bottom out at per-facet planes (same terminal fallback as today), cost bounded by the tree size.

## Your crux question: subdivide vs. give up

I'd argue the distinction mostly **dissolves** if you set the policy up right. Two observations:

- **Subdividing too often only costs time** (bounded, logarithmic). Committing to freeform too early costs *correctness* — the TODO has measured twice that a B-spline validates happily over genuinely-analytic supports ("the freeform patch validates over these faces is not evidence the region is freeform"). The asymmetry says: default to subdividing; give up only at the size floor, or into the freeform tail.
- **Don't gate the split decision on residual magnitude.** The project's history is littered with non-monotonic residual-threshold responses (the `acceptance_tol` removal, the facet-scaled-growth refutation). `REFIT_GIVE_UP` survives only as a give-up-on-augmenting test, and under recursion its role is just "fall through to the next rung, then split".

So: per node, run the DOF ladder (jet-ordered, as now). If a rung validates → commit. Else split. At the size floor → per-facet plane or the freeform handoff. The one prune worth having for the NURBS worst case is *jet homogeneity*: a node whose curvature field is smooth and generic-bi-curved, where every analytic rung has failed, is a freeform candidate — stop descending and hand the whole subtree to 2.3(e). That's "jet as prior, not gate", consistent with the codebase's philosophy, and its unreliability on coarse meshes costs only time.

## The three wrinkles your sketch doesn't cover (the honest additions)

1. **Split needs its merge half.** Real surfaces get diced across sibling leaves (a 12-face plane spanning two nodes; a fillet strip across five). This is classic split-and-merge: adjacent leaves whose *union* validates as one freshly-fit surface merge, bottom-up. The merge test already exists — it's exactly `merge_regions`' "refit the union and revalidate" (repair.rs:459+). Note this also handles over-splitting within one surface for free.
2. **Identifiability inverts.** Today the flood gives global support first and `MIN_AXIAL_SWEEP_DEG` checks it after. In the tree, a sub-node of a real torus legitimately sweeps 5° and fits a garbage-R torus that *validates locally*. So per-node, the sweep guards must become "defer, don't reject": small-sweep fits stay uncommitted merge candidates; identifiability is checked at merge/commit time, when the union's sweep is large. This is the main conceptual rework — validation semantics unchanged, but "reject" becomes "not yet".
3. **Junction-row ownership still needs a terminal pass.** Leaves straddling a tangent junction bottom out at one facet (= a plane), which is the medium-model's "~14 facets survive as per-facet planes" failure mode. Keep `resolve_claims`' logic as a final absorption: leftover leaves join a neighbouring committed surface they validate on (DOF-first preference), else per-facet plane. This is also where item A's locus machinery plugs in for boundary precision — subdivision doesn't fix the 4-surplus-surfaces problem on its own.

And two things to hold onto from the current design: children must be **mesh-contiguous** (the seam assumption — boundaries must remain edge chains; a spatial octree would violate this, a graph bisection preserves it), and the **B-spline stays out of the internal-node ladder** (last resort only, at pruned subtrees or the 2.3(e) tail).

- `bspline_blister`/`saddle` (the freeform fit itself failing) — orthogonal.
- The NURBS worst case is *bounded*, but fast only if the jet-homogeneity prune fires.

This is the divisive dual of VSA's agglomerative Lloyd iteration — same "alternate fit/assignment to a fixpoint" core, top-down instead of bottom-up. It's a *smaller* change than C: it replaces `compete_region`'s seed loop and keeps the ladder, `fit_validate`, `resolve_claims`, and the freeform tail intact, whereas C rewrites 2.3(b)–2.4 wholesale. ## Why the arithmetic is better than "one face = plane, two = cylinder"

Facet **normals** are exact in this pipeline (facets are exactly planar; the jet is what's unreliable — normals aren't the jet). And there's a lovely geometric fact the codebase already half-knows: the chord of a circular arc has its normal pointing exactly radially at the arc midpoint. So for a cylinder tessellated in arc-step quads, **every facet normal is an exact radial direction**, and for two facets at different arc positions:

**axis = n₁ × n₂** — exact, regardless of how coarse the arc steps are.

Then the circle fit in the perpendicular plane uses *vertices*, which lie on the true surface to ~1e-6 (your earlier point, and the B-prime measurements). Compare the measured alternative in the TODO breadcrumbs: the jet reads κ₁ = 0.023 / 0.162 / 2.2 across one exact r=0.5 arc on medium — the jet is worthless there, but n₁×n₂ is exact at any tessellation density. This is the "cheap, DOF-reducing bootstrap" the step-5 breadcrumbs were hunting for, and it's self-derived rather than borrowed — the per-seed borrowed-axis fragmentation failure (75→100 surfaces, measured twice) doesn't apply, because the axis comes from the patch's own validated members.

The same normal-line reasoning gives closed-form initializers up the ladder:

- **Sphere, 2 faces**: normal lines concur at the center (nearly — skew by chordal sag; take the common-perpendicular midpoint).
- **Torus, 4 faces** — and this is the one the TODO calls "the open case, 7 DOF with no equally cheap constraint": a torus normal line lies in the meridian plane, i.e. *the normal line and the axis are coplanar*. Two lines (p,d) are coplanar iff (p₂−p₁)·(d₁×d₂) = 0; each facet gives one such equation in the axis (4 DOF: direction 2 + position 2), so four faces determine the axis in closed form, then R and r fall out of the normal-line geometry. That initializer didn't exist when the breadcrumbs were written, as far as I can see in the tree.

## How it maps onto the code

The change is smaller than the subdivision idea and slots into one place: replace `seed_patch` + `condition_patch` (compete.rs:1027–1180) with an **earn-your-patch** grow:

1. BFS from the seed; maintain current type + fit (closed-form only — no LM during patch construction).
2. Per frontier face: does it validate against the current fit at vtol (the existing `face_validates`)?
   - Yes → admit, refit cheaply.
   - No → try **promotion**: refit the accepted set + candidate at the next type; if *all* accepted faces validate under the promoted fit, promote and admit.
   - No type validates → the face is across a seam. Exclude it. That's the boundary found, not a fit failure.
3. Hand the patch + type + parameters to `grow_from` unchanged — first LM polish happens there, flood extends to true extent, `resolve_claims` arbitrates, exactly as today.

The property you wanted is now structural: **every patch member was admitted by validation against a fit of already-admitted members**, so the patch can never straddle a junction. The blind patch's failure mode — contamination living on in the fit *parameters* — has no way to happen. And note what this does to seed quality: a margin seed's first ring includes faces across the junction, which fail admission and get excluded, so growth proceeds into the interior. The 323 NO GROWTH seeds mostly become productive; seed ordering (`neighbor_jet_mismatch`) stops mattering.

**What retires:** `SEED_PATCH_CAP`, `MAX_PATCH_DEPTH`, `PATCH_RADII`, `PATCH_TRIM_ROUNDS`, `MIN_CONDITION_FACES`, all of `condition_patch` (the patch is clean by construction — no trimming needed), and probably `MAX_TORUS_STARTS` (multiple flood starts exist because the fit patch was unreliable). Each is on the TODO's "prize, quantified" list.

## The hard cases, named honestly

**1. Genuine ambiguity → branch, don't choose.** Two same-height faces of a cylinder have normal lines intersecting *on the axis* — so a 2-face patch validates as a sphere of radius R centered there (this is the hole→sphere trap at patch scale). The answer is to grow both branches; the false branch self-prunes within 1–2 faces, because the next face at a different height is off the sphere by ≈ Δ²/2R ≈ 6e-4 mm ≫ vtol. Validation at vtol is sharp relative to tessellation steps, so false candidates die young and branching stays cheap. Jet ordering (`region_is_developable`) can prioritize branches but must not gate — same philosophy as now.

**2. The bent tube is the subtle one, and it needs a different promotion trigger.** Locally, a bend *is* a cylinder to within vtol (sag of the bend over a 2 mm patch at R=14 is ~3.6e-5 mm), so "validation failed" never fires and promotion to torus never triggers. Worse, the patch isn't straddling anything it shouldn't — it's legitimately one tube. The signal is **fit drift**: along a straight cylinder, successive refits are stationary; along a bend, the fitted axis precesses systematically as the support grows. Drift of the same-typed fit as support accumulates = wrong type; cylinder precessing → try the 4-face torus initializer on the accumulated patch. If this works, `promote_bend` (~130 lines, the `tube_jet` signature, `BEND_PATCH_DEPTH`/`BEND_PATCH_CAP`, the stall-frontier analysis) folds into the general promotion rule — 2.3(c) stops being a special case. This is the piece I'd prototype first, because it's the one with a genuinely new observable; everything else is assembly of existing parts.

**3. What it deliberately does not change — which is a feature.** Commit semantics stay exactly as today: the committed face set is a flood output, global, all-validated. So `MIN_AXIAL_SWEEP_DEG` and the identifiability guards keep their meaning (no inversion problem like the subdivision scheme has), `resolve_claims` still owns junction rows, and the medium-model per-facet-sliver problem is neither fixed nor worsened.

**4. What it does not fix.** The seed *sweep* cost (item 2): every margin face still seeds once — patches get cheaper and more reliable, but it's still O(N) seeds, and perversely, fewer NO GROWTH seeds means *more* successful grows, so this could raise runtime before the subdivision idea lowers it. And the model-selection problem (hill_with_hole's junk torus): incremental growth on a B-spline hill grows a small locally-valid cone exactly as today, because it *is* locally valid. That stays the known hard problem.

I'd do this one first — it's a fraction of the size, preserves every guard's semantics, and if it works, the subdivision scheme's per-node fitting gets a bootstrap for free. ## Assistant

Yes — and the facet-space version of your idea is a well-mapped piece of mathematics. But its value hinges entirely on one question: **what feeds the edge map**. Let me take the pieces in order.

## The translation to facet-space is direct (and cheap)

Your image-processing pipeline has exact named analogs on the dual graph (faces = nodes, adjacency = edges):

- **"Blur the edge data and find points far from edges"** = the **distance transform** (grassfire transform). On a graph it's multi-source Dijkstra from edge faces, weighted by centroid gaps — which, note, is in *millimetres*, not hops. That matters here: `PATCH_RADII` exists precisely because hop counts lie on graded meshes (three hops walk ~18 mm along a fillet chain swept with 6 mm facets). A geodesic distance field has no such problem, and it's parameter-free — no blur radius, no iteration count, no λ.
- **"Local maxima far from edges"** = maxima of the distance field, i.e. the **medial axis** in facet-space. (Small sign correction to your description: if you blur the edge map itself, maxima sit *at* edges — you want maxima of distance-to-edge, equivalently minima of diffused edginess. Same object.)
- **"Seeds at the maxima"** = farthest-first / maximin seeding (Gonzalez's k-center traversal). It has a real guarantee: each successive seed maximizes distance from all boundaries and all previous seeds, so the sequence is provably spread — a much better answer than `neighbor_jet_mismatch`, which is a noisy jet-based *proxy* for exactly this quantity.

So as a seed-ordering field: sound, standard, and a strict improvement on what `compete_region` does today.

## The catch: the edge map is the hard part, and one source of it is measured dead

Three possible sources, in order of trustworthiness:

**1. Sharp edges — exact and free, but blind to the junctions that matter.** Stage 2.2 already detects sharp edges, and regions are connected components over non-sharp edges (`regions.rs`). A distance field from region boundaries is computable in one Dijkstra pass. But regions are *defined* as tangent-connected interiors — the tangent junctions you actually want distance from are invisible to this source. On stepped_block, one 26122-face region contains all 71 surfaces; distance-from-sharp-boundary tells you nothing about where the fillets are.

**2. Curvature-based tangent-edge detection — measured dead, do not re-derive.** This is the 2026-07-20 pivot in TODO: no threshold separates real junctions from real curvature gradients. And there's a geometric reason it's worse than "noisy": at a tangent junction between a fillet and a plane, the fillet's arc steps *approach* the plane's slope, so the dihedral **dips** toward zero exactly at the seam. The junction is locally the *smoothest* place on the mesh — an anti-edge. Every local geometric field (jet, dihedral, curvature-step) inherits some version of this, which is why detection-as-gate kept failing.

**3. Validation-failure frontiers — exact, and you already own the machine that produces them.** From the incremental-grow idea: when the earn-your-patch grow excludes a face, that face is across a seam. The excluded frontier *is* the tangent junction row, discovered as a side effect of fitting, with no threshold anywhere. This is the only tangent-edge map in the system that's exact.

## The synthesis: distance field with feedback, not precomputation

Putting those together, the right construction isn't "detect edges → distance transform → seed", it's a loop:

1. Initialize distance sources = sharp boundaries (exact, free).
2. Seed at the **max-distance unexplained face** (farthest-first).
3. Grow with the earn-your-patch mechanism; commit the flood.
4. Mark the grow's *failure frontier* as new distance sources; update the field (incremental multi-source Dijkstra — cheap).
5. Repeat until nothing is unexplained.

This replaces the jet-mismatch ordering with a geometric ordering, and every iteration's seed is maximally far from everything known to contaminate a fit — sharp edges, plus tangent seams discovered by previous grows. No detector, no threshold, no new tuned constants.

**And it yields a principled answer to TODO item 2's actual question** ("how much of the 26122-face seed sweep does useful work"). The useful-work criterion: a seed can only claim new territory if its conditioning ball contains an *unclaimed* face. So maintain distance-to-nearest-unclaimed-face; skip any seed whose conditioning radius is smaller than that distance. That's a strict generalization of today's `interior_of_claims` (which checks only the radius-1 neighbourhood — compete.rs:420) to the true conditioning radius, and unlike a heuristic cap it's *sound*: a ball fully inside one committed surface can only refit that surface. On stepped_block fine, after the first few medial seeds commit the big surfaces, the unclaimed set collapses to junction zones, and the sweep collapses from 26K candidate seeds to the few hundred faces adjacent to leftovers. That's the runtime lever item 2 wants, derived rather than tuned.

**Bonus use — identifiability.** A face's distance value caps the support its patch can gather, which caps the DOF its fit can determine: the degenerate free-radius tori (the ones `MIN_AXIAL_SWEEP_DEG` exists to reject) are born at faces whose reachable support is a handful of faces. Distance-from-boundary gives a per-seed "max supportable DOF" prior — it can cap the promotion ladder from the incremental-grow idea (don't promote to torus where the ball can only reach 7 faces), attacking the degeneracy at seed time rather than at commit time.

## What to be honest about

- **Idea 2 changed this idea's job.** Once patches are earned face-by-face under validation, seed interiority matters much less for *correctness* — a margin seed's first ring excludes the cross-junction faces and grows inward. What distance-seeding still buys is runtime (the skip above) and fit conditioning/identifiability (medial seeds gather sweep and area). Worth having, but it's no longer load-bearing for correctness.
- **Any field computed before fitting is a prior; the only exact field comes from fitting.** If someone proposes feeding this with a jet/dihedral edge map, that's the refuted detector returning in a hat.
- **The full watershed reading is a bigger, different bet.** If you treat a continuous edginess field as topography and let basins flood until they meet, the watershed lines *are* the partition — no seeds at all. That's a legitimate third architecture (and notice: watershed ridge lines are exactly the *optimal split boundaries* for the recursive-subdivision idea — all three of your ideas converge on the same object). But it inherits watershed's classic over-segmentation on freeform regions, and it presupposes a topography whose trustworthiness is precisely what's measured dead. I'd file it under "interesting, not yet".

## Prior art worth mining

Distance/grassfire transform (Borgefors), Blum's medial axis, marker-controlled watershed (Beucher & Vincent) — the marker version is the relevant one, and your "known-interior seeds" *are* the markers; farthest-first traversal (Gonzalez 1985); and your "blur then climb to maxima" is literally **mean-shift** mode-finding, whose mesh-segmentation cousins exist. Mesh-saliency literature also computes geodesic distance-to-boundary on dual graphs routinely.

# Rework plan: brepper toward structural robustness

## The thesis in one paragraph

The measured fragility of brepper concentrates in exactly one place: **stage 2.3(b)'s seeding**, and the pile of ~34 constants that exist because tangent seams are invisible to local geometry. Everything upstream (stage 1, sharp-edge detection → regions, the DOF ladder, `fit_validate`, the validation primitives) and everything downstream (claim arbitration, stage 3 assembly, the `--compare` harness) is principled and load-bearing. So this plan does **not** rewrite the pipeline. It replaces the blind-patch seeding mechanism with fits that are *earned under validation* rather than *grabbed and repaired*, makes seed exploration distance-guided so its cost scales with boundary-zone size rather than region size, deletes whatever is then measured inert on an expanded corpus, and quarantines the two genuine research problems (freeform fit conditioning; analytic-vs-B-spline model selection) behind explicit measurement gates. A partition-level rewrite (recursive subdivision / watershed) is held in reserve and only started with the user, with data, if the earlier phases leave the goals unmet.

## Phase 1 — Earned patches (the core rework)

**Goal:** replace the blind seed patch with a patch *grown face-by-face under validation*, promoting surface type as accumulated DOF allow. A patch that can never straddle a junction by construction removes the root cause the step-5 notes document — the contamination that lives in fit *parameters*.

**Build:**
1. **Closed-form initializers from exact facet normals** (LM is only for polish, in the existing `grow_from`):
   - Plane: one face (exact).
   - Cylinder: axis = n₁ × n₂ from two faces with non-parallel normals + `fit_circle_for_axis` (exists). Guard: only normalize the cross product above a numerical-conditioning floor; otherwise keep growing as plane until a better pair arrives.
   - Sphere: normal-line intersection from two faces (common-perpendicular midpoint).
   - Conical: existing normal-covariance machinery — sound now because the patch is clean.
   - Torus: derive the 4-face normal-line-coplanarity system (a torus's normal lines lie in the meridian planes, hence are coplanar with the axis). **Time-box this.** Unit-test on synthetic tori to ~1e-9 before integrating. If the derivation or conditioning doesn't pan out, *skip it* — direct torus seeding stays as today (`torus_candidate`), and Phase 1 is not blocked.
2. **The earn loop:** BFS from the seed; admit a frontier face iff it validates the current fit at `vtol`; on failure, attempt **promotion** in ladder order (plane → conical/sphere, jet-ordered as today → torus), refitting the accepted set + candidate and requiring *all* accepted faces to validate; if nothing validates, the face is across a seam — exclude it. **Branch** on genuine ambiguity (e.g., the 2-face cylinder/sphere trap): false branches should self-prune within a couple of faces because validation at `vtol` is sharp relative to tessellation steps.
3. **Cylinder→torus promotion via fit drift.** A bend is locally a valid cylinder, so validation never triggers promotion. The signal: along a straight cylinder successive refits are stationary; along a bend the fitted axis *precesses systematically*. Test drift as *sign-consistency* of successive axis deltas, not magnitude — if it needs a magnitude constant, **stop and ask**. Success here is what later allows deleting `promote_bend`; if it doesn't work, keep `promote_bend` and move on.
4. Hand patch + type + parameters to the **unchanged** `grow_from` flood/refit/commit/`resolve_claims` path. Commit semantics deliberately do not change: every guard (`MIN_AXIAL_SWEEP_DEG`, torus-ratio, `MIN_*_FACES`) keeps its exact meaning.

**Delete (after ablation):** `seed_patch` jet filtering, `condition_patch`, `SEED_PATCH_CAP`, `MAX_PATCH_DEPTH`, `PATCH_RADII`, `PATCH_TRIM_ROUNDS`, `MIN_CONDITION_FACES`.

**Assumption checks, in order, before building further:**
- *Axis-initializer quality across provenances.* On Parasolid quad strips, cylinder chord normals are exactly radial → n₁×n₂ exact. On OCCT Delaunay interiors this is *not* guaranteed. Measure axis error against reference cylinders across the Phase-0 corpus. If median error exceeds ~1° on some provenance, the initializer degrades to "covariance-axis over the earned patch" for that case — still clean, still not blind. **If even that underperforms the blind patch on any provenance, stop and ask** — the core premise needs re-examination.
- *Branch lifetime.* Instrument: branches should die within ~2 faces. If they persist, validation isn't as sharp as believed — stop and ask before the cost model changes.
- *NO GROWTH count* on the corpus should collapse toward zero.

**Acceptance:** probe breakdowns equal-or-better on all models (stepped_block fine ≥ today's 75-surface state, medium not worse); suite green; constant count down by the deleted family.

**Explicitly NOT fixed by this phase — do not chase them here:** the 4 surplus surfaces on fine (boundary precision), medium's gap, hill_with_hole's junk torus (model selection), runtime (Phase 2).

## Phase 2 — Seed exploration: ordering and bounded cost

**Goal:** the 103 s question ("how much of the 26,122-face sweep does useful work") gets a *derived* answer: only seeds whose reachable ball contains an unclaimed face can do useful work.

**Build, profile first:**
1. **Profile the runtime** (patch construction vs. LM fits vs. floods vs. claim resolution). Apply the cheapest fix that addresses the measured distribution; do not build the full machinery below if the profile says the cost is elsewhere. Report the profile to the user regardless.
2. **Distance field:** geodesic distance-to-region-boundary per face (multi-source Dijkstra, centroid-gap weights — millimetres, not hops; graded meshes make hop counts lie). Seed order: **farthest-first**, replacing `neighbor_jet_mismatch`.
3. **Duplicate abort:** a seed claimed by surface S whose minimal-patch ball lies entirely within S can only refit S — skip it (topological rule, strict generalization of `interior_of_claims`, no numeric tolerance).
4. **Frontier feedback (only if the profile says ordering still matters):** grow-failure frontiers (faces excluded by Phase-1 earns) become new distance sources for subsequent ordering. This is the exact tangent-edge map, discovered by fitting, never detected.

**Acceptance:** runtime on stepped_block fine and the sweep substantially reduced; **results bit-identical or every diff explained** — ordering must not change output. If it does, that order-dependence is itself the fragility we're eliminating: stop and surface it.

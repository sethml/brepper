# Fusion 360 re-exports (Phase 0.5a)

Generated by `tools/fusion/BrepperReexport.py` from Fusion **2704.1.36** on
2026-07-30 — see `tools/fusion/README.md` to re-run, and
`fusion_export_log.txt` for the per-export record including the deviation
readbacks.

A third tessellator over references that already live in `tests/`: Parasolid
strips (Onshape) and constrained Delaunay (OCCT, `tests/remesh/`) are the other
two. The reference STEP is unchanged in every case, so a behaviour difference is
attributable to the mesh alone. `sources.tsv` records which reference each STL
came from; `tools/sweep.sh` reads it in preference to any naming rule.

## Densities are numeric, and identical to what tests/remesh asks of OCCT

`<model>_fusion_{coarse,medium,fine}.stl` at **surfaceDeviation 0.5 / 0.1 /
0.02 mm** and **normalDeviation 30 / 15 / 6°** — the same pairs `tests/remesh`
requests from OCCT. Fusion versus OCCT is therefore a controlled comparison at
identical requested tolerances rather than a comparison of two vendors' preset
names, and the deviations are stated inputs Phase 0.7's estimator cross-check can
use directly.

**A nominal tolerance is not comparable across tessellators.** For the same
request Fusion emits roughly half the triangles OCCT does — 2948 against 6464 at
medium on `stepped_block_toroidal`, 17598 against 35596 at fine. Worth knowing
before treating any "0.1 mm mesh" as a controlled quantity.

Both deviations are set because only one of them binds: sweeping
surfaceDeviation from 0.5 mm to 0.005 mm on a sphere changed nothing while
normalDeviation stayed at 15°. On curved primitives the **angular** tolerance
determines the mesh.

## Ground truth: 31 of 31

Fusion can heal or reparameterize geometry on STEP import, which would silently
invalidate a pair. It does not: every export's vertices lie on the **original**
reference surfaces to within **1.8e-7 to 7.3e-6 mm**. `tools/verify_pairs.sh`
reports the rate.

## What the pipeline does with them

15 of 31 pass end-to-end `--compare`; the 16 that do not carry their numbers in
`tests/stage4_integration.rs`. Three results are worth reading here.

**Four models are rejected at stage 1, not by the decomposition.** A mesh
exported at 0.5 mm chordal deviation has facet-centroid sag of 0.51–0.83 mm,
over the 0.4 mm default `--surface-tolerance`. Their vertices are impeccable
(1.8e-7 to 5.4e-6 mm) — this is the tolerance regime refusing an ordinary coarse
export, and it is a Phase 3 datum rather than a fitting failure. It only became
visible once the centroid check stopped measuring against a solid (`src/compare.rs`).

**`rounded_cube_10_r2` reproduces the random corpus's collapse.** At medium
(684 triangles) it gives **9 surfaces — 0 planes, 0 cylinders, 8 spheres, 1
B-spline** — failing volume by 1.94e-2. That is the exact signature the seeded
`rand_filleted_box` models showed (0/0/8/1 against a reference 6/12/8), so the
collapse is not a property of generated geometry: the corpus's canonical fillet
model does it too, at an ordinary export density. `part_rounded_cube` medium
collapses the same way to 3 surfaces. Both are exact at fine.

**`stepped_block_toroidal` still fails on all three tessellators**, but Fusion
fine reaches 82 surfaces against a reference 71 — better than OCCT fine's 97
with half the triangles, and behind Parasolid fine's 75 with a third of them.
The surplus is planes in every case. See TODO 0.5a for the full table.
